\begin{abstract}

Parameterizing baseline proteotoxic stress via experimental urea-denaturation shifts ($\Delta\Delta G \in [0.00, 4.00]\,\mathrm{kcal/mol}$ across \texttt{WT}, \texttt{R273H}, \texttt{R248Q}, \texttt{R175H}, and \texttt{Y220C}) and grounding sequence-encoded aggregation propensity in recent biophysical literature (\texttt{EuropePMC: 42840663}, \texttt{42143149}), we identify the metabolic inflow regime separating sustained chaperone buffering from energetic depletion. \section{Coupled Discrete Energetic-Proteotoxic Dynamics}
For a p53 variant with folding free-energy shift $\Delta\Delta G \ge 0$, baseline proteotoxic stress is bounded by $s_0(\Delta\Delta G) = \min(0.95,\, s_{\mathrm{basal}} + \alpha\,\Delta\Delta G)$. At each discrete step $t \in \{1,\dots,30\}$, available metabolic budget $E_t$ receives basal inflow $E_{\mathrm{inflow}}$ and funds chaperone activity $a_t \le a_{\mathrm{target}}$ at unit cost $\kappa_{\mathrm{chap}}$, reducing effective stress to $s_{\mathrm{eff},t} = s_0\max(0,\, 1 - \gamma_{\mathrm{chap}} a_t)$ while incurring stress-induced energetic drain $\kappa_{\mathrm{stress}} s_{\mathrm{eff},t}$:
\begin{equation}
E_{t+1} = \max\Bigl(0,\; E_t + E_{\mathrm{inflow}} - \kappa_{\mathrm{chap}} a_t - \kappa_{\mathrm{stress}} s_{\mathrm{eff},t}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
