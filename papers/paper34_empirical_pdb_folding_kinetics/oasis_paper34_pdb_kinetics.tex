\section{Physical Clarification and Topological Contact-Order Mapping}
In macroscopic thermal dispersion (such as boiling water atomized into sub-zero air), rapid freezing arises from surface-area amplification and convective/evaporative heat loss rather than a violation of energy balance. In aqueous two-state protein folding (\texttt{arXiv:2503.23280}, \texttt{arXiv:2604.04677}), experimental rates $\ln k_f$ are governed by native topological complexity measured via Relative Contact Order ($\mathrm{RCO}$). Projecting conformational dynamics onto a 1D reaction coordinate (\texttt{arXiv:2607.20504}), the discrete contraction rate is parameterized as $k_{\mathrm{fold}}(\mathrm{RCO}) = k_0 \exp(-\gamma\,\mathrm{RCO})$ with $k_0 = 0.95$ and $\gamma = 6.2$.
