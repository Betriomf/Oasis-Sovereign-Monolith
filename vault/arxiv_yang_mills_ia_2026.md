## 1. Últimos Papers de IA / Machine Learning aplicados a Yang–Mills

### [1] The Continuum Limit of the Deep Linear Network
- **Autores:** Catherine Eva Pfaff, Karen Uhlenbeck
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-10-31` (Actualizado `2026-10-31`) | **Categorías:** `math.DG, cs.LG`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00503v1) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00503v1.pdf)
- **Resumen:** The Deep Linear Network (DLN) is a simple version of a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) in which the activation function is the identity and the bias vector is the zero vector. We examine the behavior of the model when the number of weight matrices, which is finite in the original model, goes to infinity. We show that a plausible continuum limit is a dimensionally reduced version of the Yang-Mills equation on the torus, revealing an unexpected geometric interpretation of infinite-depth DLNs. While we do not prove this rigorously, it is a very pretty calculation. It also gives some insight into several features of the construction. We note that the choices of boundary conditions on the ends of the discrete series survive better in the continuum limit. Also, the regularizing functional which uses the moment map survives nicely in the continuum limit, whereas the regularizing functional using the size of approximating terms has no sensible limit. This surely is useful information for the choices we make in the finite-dimensional theory, and might also be useful in the more complicated MLP setting.

### [2] Ergodic topology sampling toward the continuum with Stochastic Normalizing Flows
- **Autores:** Claudio Bonanno, Andrea Bulgarelli, Elia Cellini, Alessandro Nada et al.
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-10-14` (Actualizado `2026-10-14`) | **Categorías:** `hep-lat, cond-mat.stat-mech, cs.LG, hep-ph`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20747v1) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20747v1.pdf)
- **Resumen:** In this work we provide numerical evidence that topological freezing in $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ Yang-Mills theory can be mitigated with a flow-based approach, as tested at lattice spacings as fine as 0.03~fm. We do so by analyzing the scaling of the costs of a computational strategy based on the combination of open boundary conditions and Stochastic Normalizing Flows. We show that the integrated autocorrelation time of the squared topological charge and the statistical uncertainty on the topological susceptibility remain under control throughout the explored parameter range with currently available computational resources. This marks an important step toward the use of even more efficient flow-based architectures for ergodic sampling of topology in state-of-the-art lattice gauge theory calculations.

### [3] Learning Scattering Amplitudes with Transformer Reinforcement Learning
- **Autores:** Philip Velie, Piyush Jha, Vijay Ganesh, Aishik Ghosh
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-09-26` (Actualizado `2026-10-31`) | **Categorías:** `hep-th`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32096v2) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32096v2.pdf)
- **Resumen:** We introduce a transformer reinforcement learning algorithm that learns to solve high loop-level scattering amplitudes in planar N = 4 Super Yang-Mills theory. Our algorithm improves on previous transformer-only results by incorporating previously derived symmetries and relationships into the learning algorithm. This results in a greatly decreased fraction of the solution that needs to be known a priori to solve the entire problem. An additional benefit is that our algorithm also ensures that every output obeys the set of known relationships. Rather than predicting all coefficients independently, the model proposes assignments that are propagated through exact linear relations, while MCTS searches over assignments when propagation alone is insufficient. This is crucial to the generalization of machine learning approaches to higher loops, as without this, there is no way to overcome the factorially scaling of state sizes and compare to results derived via other methods. Using the symbology representation of the form factor, we frame the problem as learning a mapping between discrete sequences and integer coefficients.

### [4] Calculating Axion-Matter Couplings in String Theory
- **Autores:** Joshua N. Benabou, Naomi Gendler, Thomas R. Harvey, Jakob Moritz
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-09-17` (Actualizado `2026-10-05`) | **Categorías:** `hep-th, hep-ph`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20911v2) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20911v2.pdf)
- **Resumen:** In this work we calculate, for the first time, the dominant perturbative contributions to axion--matter couplings explicitly in string theory. Concretely, we compute these couplings in Calabi--Yau compactifications of heterotic string theory where the vector bundle is a sum of line bundles. They are expressed as overlap integrals requiring the Ricci-flat metric, the Hermitian Yang-Mills bundle metric, and the matter field harmonic forms. We compute these by solving the corresponding coupled differential equations with neural networks. Our results establish a characteristic hierarchy of four-dimensional couplings: axions couple to fermions as $\mathcal{L} \supset c_ψ\,\frac{\partial_μa}{f_a}\, ψ^\dagger \barσ^μψ$ with $c_ψ$ an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ function of Kähler moduli for the model-dependent axions, while the corresponding coupling for the model-independent axion is suppressed by $α_{\rm YM}$ at the ultraviolet matching scale. The model-dependent axions thus couple to fermions similarly as DFSZ-like four-dimensional axions, whereas the model-independent axion's couplings are loop-suppressed as in KSVZ-like constructions. The methods developed here apply directly to the large class of heterotic line bundle models with realistic spectra, and can be adapted to type II constructions.

## 2. Últimos Papers sobre el Salto de Masa (Yang–Mills Mass Gap)

### [MG-1] SU(2) Yang-Mills: Spectral Bounds, Vacuum Concentration, and Criteria for the Continuum Limit
- **Autores:** Aleksandar Mihajlovic
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-11-08` | **Categorías:** `math-ph`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11534v1) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11534v1.pdf)
- **Resumen:** A continuum mass-gap argument must preserve nonzero renormalized states and a positive spectral estimate while the spatial cutoff is removed and physical volume grows. We develop quantitative results that separate these requirements. Gauge reduction, reflection positivity and attributed strong-coupling estimates give physical-transfer bounds with nontrivial local sectors. At fixed spatial cutoff and positive coupling, we construct subsequential infinite-volume physical-time dynamics from local history correlations, with nonconstant gauge-invariant observables. For finite spatial boxes, explicit bounds apply to the entire physical vacuum complement; finite-matrix criteria include all omitted representations. Vacuum concentration gives extensive entropy bounds, quantitative fidelity obstructions for hard electric cutoffs, and necessary growth of retained representation spaces. A sharper local comparison yields complete bounds on fixed discarded-link sectors, while collective projection and Schur estimates isolate the additional input needed for a volume-uniform gap. The work also certifies a finite three-link conditioning functional through Wilson coupling 0.354, with its physical interpretation conditional on the stated block-comparison hypotheses. Exact blocking, corrected effective-potential theorems and a representation-by-representation two-dimensional benchmark clarify which approximations preserve the relevant observables. Published glueball measurements retain their original attribution; their reanalysis and the reproduced winding-channel and replica studies have explicitly statistical scope. Complete derivations and reproducible calculations support a set of certified benchmarks and continuum transfer criteria. A nontrivial four-dimensional spatial-cutoff construction and a positive spectral bound along it are not established; no exact continuum mass is asserted.

### [MG-2] Non-Hermitian Yang--Mills Connections with Vanishing Chern Classes
- **Autores:** Guangzhen Ren
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-09-30` | **Categorías:** `math.DG`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39961v2) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39961v2.pdf)
- **Resumen:** We construct non-flat non-Hermitian Yang--Mills connections with zero Einstein constant on smoothly trivial rank-two bundles over compact Kähler surfaces, with positive harmonic metrics and stable induced and adjoint holomorphic bundles. Thus vanishing Chern classes do not force flatness even under stability on both sides. The local model comes from a known complex anti-self-dual ansatz; the stable descents yield explicit global moduli phenomena. For a fixed stable bundle, the Kaledin--Verbitsky map contracts a complex line containing flat and non-flat points in the connected component of the Hermitian--Einstein connection. Contraction also occurs in the two-sided stable locus. The pair of holomorphic projections has a fibre containing both flat and non-flat points. An energy identity and a spectral-gap estimate quantify local flatness with the induced holomorphic structure fixed.

### [MG-3] Non-abelian quantum cellular automata: $1{+}1$-dimensional $SU(2)$ Yang--Mills with fermions
- **Autores:** Dogukan Bakircioglu, Pablo Arrighi
- **Fecha:** Publicado `2026-09-03` | **Categorías:** `quant-ph, hep-lat`
- **Enlaces:** [Abstract arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30285v2) | [PDF Directo](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30285v2.pdf)
- **Resumen:** This work provides a digital quantum simulation scheme for $1{+}1$-dimensional $SU(2)$ Yang--Mills theory with Dirac fermions. It takes the form of a quantum circuit, infinitely repeating across space and time with $Δ_t=Δ_x=\varepsilon$, whose wires follow lightlike propagation. The construction mirrors the logic of the standard quantum field theory approach, transposed to the discrete setting. Namely, we start from the Dirac quantum walk, restore $SU(2)$ gauge symmetry by introducing the gauge field, lift the walk to a multi-particle QCA while preserving fermionic anticommutation, and equip the gauge field with its own dynamics. Rather than relying on Clebsch--Gordan decompositions, we use the pointwise-product structure of gauge-link updates, which yields self-contained proofs of unitarity and gauge covariance in quantum-computing notation. The construction provides an explicit algorithmic formulation of the theory, whose continuum limit we discuss.
